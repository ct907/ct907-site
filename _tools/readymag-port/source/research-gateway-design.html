NLB RESEARCH GATEWAY

2022

Summary

Singapore's National Library Board(NLB) offers a vast collection of reference resources for researchers. However, these resources were not prominent or discoverable. Our team revamped the search experience to focus on fast access and content discovery for researchers. This was done by:

1. Consolidate previously separated repository sites into one gateway

2. Standardize the search experience for different item categories

3. Reimagine how NLB’s vast list of library collections are browsed.

Project Scope

Build a consolidated Research Gateway, focused on surfacing NLB's vast collection of resources.

User Research

To determine how researchers - the core user group - uses NLB resources, we’ve performed user interviews, competitive studies and stakeholder workshops as primary research.


While the type of content and subject might differ, we’ve discovered that researchers perform either Focused Searches or Exploratory Searches.

1. Focused Search Users 🔍 📋

Users who do Focused Searches hunt for the most relevant content as fast as possible.

Focused Searchers put in the closest keywords or categories most efficiently, then filter deeply into the specific subject matter of their choosing. To save time and mental effort, they also crave a common, predictable flow when it comes to search and filtering methods for their research.

2. Exploratory Search Users 🔭 ✨

Users who do Exploratory Searches like to discover new things within their field of knowledge.

Researchers will follow a general direction to find new connections and insights in their mental map of the subject matter. Exploratory Researchers search laterally across all available resources, and tend to exhaust all possible filtering options when searching.

Problem Statement

NLB's existing search engine uses 2 methods to find resources.



1. Focused Keyword Search

Keyword search in NLB's existing search engine. However it does not recommend or promote related content for Exploratory flows.



2. Exploratory Resource Browsing

Find the directory for NLB resources and collections, then enter a microsite listing resources for only one specific format. It does not allow cross-searching between microsites.

Overall, a Disconnected Experience

1. Content Navigation 🧭

NLB resources were spread out in 10+ different microsites.

Each microsite functions as a specialized repository for a specific library item type, but do not share resources or cross link between each other when users perform searches. (E.g. BookSG, MusicSG, Infopedia)

Metaphorically, items were locked behind numerous doors, each door only unlocked by remembering its name. This adds unnecessary cognitive load to researchers who prioritize quick information retrieval in their work.






2. A Fragmented Experience 🎭

This resulted in a fragmented experience throughout NLB’s offerings.

The way individual sites looked and functioned were inconsistent, e.g. using sorting methods and terms that were specific to the type of item being searched. Each microsite functioned as its own brand, operating without a connected, seamless experience when users browse NLB’s offerings.








3. Content Visibility 👀

Each microsite curates it's own library content separetely.


These collections aid users in the discovery of content and facilitate further learning. Siloed behind different microsites, the collections lacked a central repository to aid in discovery.


The collections are also considered a class of objects by themselves, and lacked a central sorting method to organize them for easier browsing.

Our Approach

Enhance Content Structure and Content Visibility.

Let users perform Focused Searches and Exploratory Searches effectively.

1. Enhance Content Visibility

1A. Enhancing Collections

To increase visibility and engagement with the collections while respecting the original context, an extra layer of curation was added on top of the collection, called Stories. To group similar Collections together, library staff can now place them under a Story that goes by a specific theme, era or topic etc.


We've also added a Collections Directory section to consolidate all Collections from separate microsites, while featuring Stories on the first fold. This lets the Collections retain their respective names but still get surfaced to interested users.


In our testing, researchers found that the Collections page is visually engaging and has potential.



1B. Reusable Templates

To further improve content engagement and visibility, a system of templates was built specifically to showcase Stories and Collections.

These template pages were built with modular components, allowing content managers to swap out components to fit future content.They come in 4 different content styles.


User testing with researchers also proved positive, 80% of users felt the template pages are more engaging and showcases the topic more clearly.

2. Consolidated Search and Filter Sidebar Behaviour

2A. Deep Filtering

To ease content navigation during search, search filters and extra resources are placed in an omnipresent sidebar.

The sidebar expands to allow deep filtering of search results while still displaying all filtering options at a glance for fast switching. In our user testing, researchers found the sidebar intuitive, but needs more time to be familiarized with it.



2B. Consistent Filtering

In our analysis, 50% of resources do not offer a standardized method of filtering their content. On the sidebar, we removed non-essential filters and feature the most commonly available filters for all items like Availability, Format, Category and Date for easier access.


A highly requested feature in our interviews was to include a Glossary page for researchers to quickly see all available filtering options at a glance. This is placed in the More Filters section.

2C. Consistent Results

The item detail pages shown below are the end goal for researcher user journeys. These pages consolidate all possible content types provided by NLB and follows a standard layout for consistency.

Convenient features such as quick links for saving favourite items, item citation and downloads are placed at the first fold for easy access.

Learning Points

From this project, I have learned that designing for a seasoned user has other considerations to fit their needs. It isn't always about clean minimal interfaces.




1. Trust the seasoned users 📊⚙️

Quality-of-life features that let them save time are welcomed, e.g. letting the sidebar be persistent when browsing search results or minimizing clicks when selecting filters by using hover states more.



2. More features, please! 📚📲

As long as there is proper onboarding, seasoned users don't mind seeing additional options/features if it benefits their work. This was especially true for users like researchers who want to know the depth and breadth your product offers.
