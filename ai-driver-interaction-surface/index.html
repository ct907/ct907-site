EXPLORATION

2019

AI DRIVer Interaction SUrfaces

Visual exploration of AI-Passenger interactions inside driverless vehicle interiors.

How would an AI Driver convey friendliness and trustworthiness through non-physical means? We explored using coded visuals projected on the largest available interior surfaces of vehicles - the roof and windows.


Part of a larger UX research pilot study, these patterns are constructed based on a visual interpretation of Speech Act Theory.

WINDSHIELD SURFACE


In driverless vehicles, the windshield replaces the function of the dashboard, becoming the main information display for passengers.


To enhance the perception of driving safety and trustworthiness in a driverless setting, a system of graphics and patterns were used to alleviate anxiety in first time encounters with AI drivers.


Detection overlays on other vehicles and road users were explored, allowing passengers to see what the AI driver sees.

ROOF SURFACE

The roof of the vehicle was imagined as the AI driver's main display surface to display intentions and emotional affects in the form of a synthesized glyph visual language. These glyphs animate along with the AI driver's spoken words, displaying contextual clues akin to human body language to give the AI additional modes of expression.

The AI visual language used in this scenario was first imagined as simple geometric arrangements, centered around a core pattern representing the main thought or intention, with surrounding elements augmenting the intensity of the core pattern.


The final designs of this initial exploration were used in a UX Research project exploring passenger interactions with AI drivers. The interaction surfaces were simulated in both physical and VR mockups. A live feed of visual patterns and trip information were displayed through these interaction surfaces.
