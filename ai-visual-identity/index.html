VISUAL EXPLORATION

2020

AI VISUAL identity

Visual language forms inspired by Speech Act Theory

This visual exploration aims to construct an AI visual identity based on a visual interpretation of John Searle’s Speech Act Theory. This pattern generation method was later used in a driverless vehicle UX research project testing AI-passenger interactions.

Distrust in AI stems from unfamiliarity of their intentions and inner workings. To foster trust, an AI can make use of richer modes of communication familiar to humans to display emotional states or contextual information, similar to facial expressions and body language in humans.

Theories of human-human communication such as Speech Act Theory were explored to adapt the fundamentals of communication to enhance AI-Human interactions.


Speech Act Theory deconstructs the act of spoken words into three components and 4 conditions. Is there a way to translate these building blocks of speech into a visual medium, allowing them accompany the spoken words by the AI?

The 3 main components of Speech Act were visualized as the components of a glyph, building blocks of a visual language.

BUILDING THE GLYPHS


1. The Locutionary Act

This is the base component of the glyph, constructed using arbitrary strokes from body movements, objects or facial expressions, reminiscent of early human writing. Examples shown were constructed from watching a dancer's movements.

2. Felicity Conditions as Modifiers

The felicity conditions of Speech Act Theory are visualized as modifiers that change the visual appearance of the glyphs to suit the energy of conversation and content. Similar to how certain body postures or hand gestures can affect the delivery and meaning of what’s spoken, the glyphs are modified in appearance and embellished with extra patterns.

These modifiers were inspired by Sign Language users being able to modify the delivery of their hand signs using different facial expressions or body gestures.

Final Output

The glyphs produced are shown as proof of concept, informing the creative direction of the motion graphics used for a driverless vehicle UX research project testing AI-passenger interactions.
